How ice dams form

Warm attic → melt → refreeze at the eaves

When heat leaks into the attic it warms the roof deck and melts the snow sitting on it. That meltwater runs down to the cold overhang at the eaves, refreezes, and builds a ridge of ice. Water then pools behind the dam and works its way under the shingles — into the sheathing, walls and ceilings. Chipping ice and running heat cables every winter treats the symptom; keeping the deck cold stops it.

01 / SEAL

Stop the heat leak

Spray foam air-seals the attic — top plates, penetrations, can lights — so warm air stays in the living space instead of warming the deck.

02 / INSULATE

Cold roof deck

A well-insulated roofline or attic floor keeps the deck near outdoor temperature, so the snow on top doesn't melt in the first place.

03 / PROTECT

Save the structure

No melt-refreeze cycle means no water backing up under the shingles into sheathing, walls and ceilings.

Questions

Common questions

What causes ice dams?

Heat leaking into the attic warms the roof deck, which melts the snow on top. The meltwater runs down to the cold eaves and overhang, refreezes, and builds a dam of ice that backs water up under the shingles. It's an insulation-and-air-sealing problem more than a roofing problem.

How does spray foam prevent ice dams?

By air-sealing and insulating the attic so heat stays in the living space and the roof deck stays cold. A cold deck means the snow doesn't melt and refreeze at the eaves — you break the cycle at its source instead of chipping ice every winter.

Isn't this a roofing fix?

Heat cables and better flashing treat symptoms; they don't stop the melt. Keeping the deck cold with air-sealing and insulation addresses the actual cause. We'll tell you straight what your roof and attic need.
