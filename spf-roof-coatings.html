Why the coating matters

The coating is the roof's sunscreen

Bare spray foam is a great insulator and air seal, but left exposed the sun's UV will break it down over time. That's what the coating is for. We spray or roll a protective elastomeric or silicone layer over the foam that seals it against UV and moisture, gives you a tough, walkable surface for service techs, and — when it eventually wears — gets recoated instead of torn off. Maintain the coating and a foam roof can keep going for decades.

Questions

Roof coatings — FAQ

What kind of coating goes on a spray foam roof?

A protective elastomeric or silicone top coat, applied to manufacturer spec. It seals the foam against UV and moisture and leaves a tough, walkable surface for service and inspections.

How often does a foam roof need to be recoated?

The coating is the wear layer, so the roof gets recoated when the coating reaches the end of its service life — you clean and recoat the same surface instead of tearing off. How long that takes varies with the coating, its thickness, sun, and foot traffic, so we inspect yours and give you a straight timeline.

Silicone or acrylic — which roof coating is better?

Both work well; the right one depends on your roof and climate. Silicone stands up to standing water and heavy UV and stays flexible, so it is a strong pick where ponding is a concern. Acrylic is cost-effective and highly reflective and does well on sloped roofs that drain and dry. We match the coating to your roof and tell you which one fits.
