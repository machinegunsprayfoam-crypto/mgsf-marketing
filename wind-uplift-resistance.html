Wind & foam roofing — FAQ

Why do roofs blow off in high wind?

Wind gets under a roof at its weak points — loose edges, seams, and fasteners — and peels it from there. A fully-adhered spray foam roof has none of those gaps, so there's nothing for the wind to catch.

Is a foam roof a good choice for a windy site in Montana or the Dakotas?

It's one of the better choices for a windy site. Spray foam bonds to the whole deck as one seamless layer with no fastener points, which gives it strong uplift resistance across MT, ND, SD and WY. We'll assess your roof and tell you straight.

Does a spray foam roof hold up to hail?

It holds up well to smaller hail — the foam and its elastomeric coating are flexible and absorb impact rather than cracking like some brittle materials. No roof is truly hail-proof, though; a severe storm can still bruise the coating. The upside is the coating is renewable, so dings can be cleaned up and recoated instead of replacing the whole roof. After a big storm we can inspect it and tell you where it stands.
