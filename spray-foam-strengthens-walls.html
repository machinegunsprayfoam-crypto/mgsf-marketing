The short answer

Rigid foam, stiffer wall

Closed-cell spray foam cures into a hard, rigid layer that bonds to the studs and sheathing. That bond adds racking stiffness — the wall's ability to resist being pushed out of square by wind and load. It won't replace engineered shear bracing, but it meaningfully stiffens the assembly, and you get that as a bonus on top of the air-seal and the insulation. Open-cell stays soft, so this benefit comes from closed-cell.

Questions

Common questions

Does spray foam actually make walls stronger?

Closed-cell foam cures rigid and bonds to the studs and sheathing, which adds racking stiffness — the wall's resistance to being pushed out of square by wind and load. It's not a substitute for engineered bracing, but it meaningfully stiffens the assembly while it insulates and air-seals.

Is that true for open-cell foam too?

No — open-cell stays soft and flexible, so the strength benefit comes from closed-cell. If added rigidity matters for your wall, that points toward closed-cell.

Why does that matter for metal buildings and barns?

Closed-cell bonds to metal panels and framing, stiffening the assembly and cutting the oil-canning flex and rattle you get in wind — on top of sealing the metal against condensation. That's a big reason it's the go-to for shops, barns and steel buildings.
