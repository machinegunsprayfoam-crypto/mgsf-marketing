Steel buildings and hard winters — foam's home turf

Williston runs on metal buildings — shops, warehouses and equipment storage across the Bakken. In a Northern Plains winter, warm air inside those steel shells hits cold panels and condenses, dripping on equipment and rusting fasteners. Closed-cell spray foam bonds to the steel as an air and vapor barrier, so the sweating stops and the building finally holds heat. Homes and pole barns get the same tight, efficient envelope. Yes. Closed-cell foam is an air and vapor barrier bonded to the steel, so warm air never reaches the cold panel to condense. That stops the drip, the rust and the wet floor at the source.
