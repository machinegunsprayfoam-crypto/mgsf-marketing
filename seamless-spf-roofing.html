Why seamless wins

One continuous layer, nowhere for water to track

Most roof leaks start at a seam, a fastener, or a flashing joint. Spray foam has none of those — it's applied as one continuous, fully-adhered layer that bonds to the whole roof deck, vertical walls, and penetrations alike. Water can't track under it, and on the rare puncture, the leak shows up right where the damage is instead of traveling across the roof. Seamless is simply a stronger way to roof.

Questions

Seamless roofing — FAQ

Where do most roof leaks start?

At seams, fasteners, and flashing joints — the connections in a roof. A spray foam roof is applied as one continuous, fully-adhered layer, so it doesn't have those connections for water to get through.

What happens if a spray foam roof gets punctured?

Because the foam is fully bonded to the deck, water can't track sideways under it — a puncture leaks right where the damage is instead of traveling across the roof. That makes it quicker to find and patch, and the coating is renewable.

Can a spray foam roof go over my existing roof?

Often, yes. SPF is lightweight and can usually be applied right over a sound existing roof — metal, flat, or low-slope — once it is cleaned and prepped, which avoids a full tear-off. If the existing roof is wet or failing underneath, that has to be dealt with first, so we inspect it and tell you straight what yours needs.
